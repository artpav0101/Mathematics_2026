### Exercise 8. Determinant of a Product

Since $\det A=3$ and $\det B=-5$,

$$
\det(AB)=\det A\det B=3(-5)=-15.
$$

Similarly,

$$
\det(BA)=\det B\det A=(-5)3=-15.
$$

The matrix $A^{-1}$ exists because $\det A=3\neq0$. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\det(A^{-1}B)
&=\det(A^{-1})\det B \\
&=\frac{1}{\det A}\det B \\
&=\frac{-5}{3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The first two determinants are equal because scalar multiplication of real numbers is commutative: $\det A\det B=\det B\det A$. This does not imply that $AB=BA$; the matrices themselves need not commute.
